## Organ-meat nutrient facts you may use (Josh 2026-11-19: "and all the nutrients facts in organ meat")
Source: USDA FoodData Central, raw beef liver vs raw beef steak, per equal weight. These describe the FOOD, not a capsule: say "ounce for ounce, liver has..." and never claim what a capsule or a daily dose delivers.
- B12: liver has roughly 40 times the B12 of a steak (about 59 vs 1.5 micrograms per 100 g). Safe spoken form: "about forty times the B12 of a steak" or "more B12 than any other food you can buy".
- Copper: liver has roughly 100 times the copper of a steak. Spoken: "a hundred times the copper of a steak".
- Vitamin A: liver is one of the richest food sources of vitamin A; steak has almost none. Spoken: "loaded with vitamin A, which steak barely has".
- Iron: liver has roughly 3 times the iron of a steak, and it's heme iron, the kind found in meat that your body takes in best. Spoken: "about three times the iron of a steak, the kind your body actually takes in".
- Folate and choline: liver is among the richest whole-food sources of both.
- Versus fruits and vegetables (Josh 2026-11-19: "don't just compare to steak, compare to fruits and veggies as well"), same USDA source, raw, per equal weight:
  - B12: fruits and vegetables have none at all; liver is the richest food source. Spoken: "no fruit or vegetable has any B12. Liver has more than any food."
  - Vitamin A: liver has roughly 6 times the vitamin A of carrots (about 4,970 vs 835 micrograms per 100 g), and it's the ready-to-use form; carrots give beta-carotene your body has to convert.
  - Iron: liver has about twice the iron of spinach (4.9 vs 2.7 mg per 100 g), and liver's is heme iron, which the body takes in far better than the iron in spinach.
  - Copper: liver has well over 100 times the copper of kale or spinach.
  - Folate: liver has more folate than spinach (about 290 vs 195 micrograms per 100 g).
  - Don't compare vitamin C or fiber: fruits and vegetables win there.
- Vita Bull contains liver, so the liver facts may be used there, tied to drive/energy, never sold as an iron product. Do not give numbers for testicle, prostate, bone marrow or collagen (no verified figures here).

## Testosterone decline (Vita Bull only; Josh 2026-11-19: "and also the testosterone decline over the last 40 years")
Verified: the Massachusetts Male Aging Study (Travison et al., Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, 2007) found men's average testosterone fell about 1% a year from 1987 to 2004, beyond normal aging, so a man today has lower testosterone than a man the same age did a generation ago. A 2020 study (Lokeshwar et al., European Urology Focus) found the same drop in young US men from 1999 to 2016.
Safe spoken forms: "Men today have lower testosterone than men the same age did thirty years ago." / "It's been dropping about one percent a year, even in young guys." / "My dad at fifty had more testosterone than I do at fifty."
Do NOT use "dropped 60% in 30 years" (the current Vital Bull page says it; it overstates the studies) or blame microplastics, seed oils, soy or doctors. One mention per script at most, said by a person who heard it, and only where it answers "why is this happening to me".
